# Expanded submission: the main functional principles

This candidate extends the seven discrete results documented in
[DISCRETE_SUBMISSION.md](DISCRETE_SUBMISSION.md) with two principal results:

| Paper | Declaration | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction (II) | `BGWLeaves.intro_II_general` | Joint walk, contour and height convergence under leaf conditioning, including lattice laws. |
| Theorem 8.1(II) | `BGWLeaves.thm_general_II` | The same joint convergence under conditioning by an offspring-mark count. |

Both use any admissible norming sequence for the specified spectrally positive
stable law and every sequence of conditioning counts tending to infinity with
positive conditioning probability. The second retains the paper's requirement
that the mark set be finite or cofinite in infinite variance. The first has no
extra non-lattice assumption. Neither is replaced by a statement about separate
marginals: the limiting excursion and height are coupled, and the height is
duplicated in the contour and height coordinates.

## Concrete statement interface

The Challenge imports only canonical Mathlib and Lean core. The custom tree
carrier, Bienaymé law, conditioning events and laws, coding maps, Skorokhod J1
distance, stable process law, normalized excursion law, height process and joint
limiting law have explicit values. Two proof-bearing typeclass constructions
are named definition holes, supplied by Solution and fixed by checked equations:

- `instFunLikeDElemRealIccOfNat`: `coe_mk` says that the evaluation of every
  path constructor is its underlying function. Every path is such a
  constructor, so this fixes the coercion; its injectivity field is a proof.
- `instMetricSpace`: `dist_eq_j1Dist` fixes its distance to the displayed J1
  formula. Mathlib's `MetricSpace.ext` shows that the distance determines the
  whole metric-space structure, including its topology and uniformity.

Together these equations uniquely determine evaluation and the J1 topology.
The Solution supplies the original instances with their proofs, using only the
three permitted axioms.

Construction proofs are additional theorem targets in `comparator.json`.
Comparator checks their statements and verifies their actual Solution proofs,
instead of requiring their proof bodies to be copied into Challenge. This also
applies to named proof fields generated by Lean while packaging a concrete
definition. These auxiliary targets add no hypotheses to the nine principal
results. The Solution imports the proved modules, never Challenge.

Small syntax helpers preserve the development's literal, order and a.e.
instance paths. They expand to canonical Mathlib expressions and introduce no
mathematical constants. Reducibility and instance attributes let the displayed
subtype coercions elaborate to the development's existing terms.

Selecting construction lemmas as checked theorem targets removes their proof
bodies from the equality comparison while retaining their kernel-checked
proofs in Solution.

## Scope and review

The seven discrete results and their corrections remain selected. Other
functional results and the Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov statements are not selected
by this configuration. The exact target list is authoritative in
`comparator.json`; supporting targets are construction checks, not additional
paper theorems advertised as principal results.

The new interface is self-reviewed against the paper's two displays and the
existing correspondence record, not independently reviewed. All original proof
sources are preserved. The earlier development audits remain applicable to
those unchanged proofs. Fresh exact comparison and mechanical evidence for the
expanded candidate is listed in [README.md](README.md).
